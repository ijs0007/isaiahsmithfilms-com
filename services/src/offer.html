Your Google listing

The panel that appears when somebody searches your name, or searches for what you sell with the word "near me" on the end.

Getting it created, getting it verified, and filling it out the way Google actually wants it filled out. Category, service areas, hours, services, photos, and the website link pointed at the page that answers what that person came for.

What it takes from you. It has to be created under your own Google login and Google verifies it against you personally, so we do that part together at your computer. Verification can take a few days before anything shows publicly.
Reviews. The best time to ask a customer for one is the day you finish their job, and almost nobody does. I will set you up to ask.

Straight with you. There are several ways to get a listing suspended, and a suspended listing is slow and painful to get back. Putting a mailbox address in the location field is one. Stuffing keywords into the business name is another, and competitors report it. I know which ones to stay away from because I read the rules properly before I set up mine.

A phone that answers when you cannot

Most people who call a small business and get voicemail do not leave one. They hang up and call the next name on the list, and you never find out.

So when a call comes in and nobody picks up, the caller gets a text from your business within seconds. They reply to it, that reply lands on your phone, and you answer when you get a minute. It is not a chatbot and nobody is pretending to be you. It is one automatic first text that holds the door open.

A new business number. For anyone still running the business off a personal cell. A real local number that belongs to the business, while your cell stays yours.
Keep the number you have. Your existing line forwards over only when nobody answers, so nothing changes for the people calling you. The text-back reaches them from the service number rather than the one they dialled.
Move your number across. Calls and texts both live on your number and the customer sees one number throughout. Cleanest for them, longest to arrange.

Straight with you. Every US business sending automated texts has to be registered with the phone carriers first, and I do that paperwork. Mine took twelve days and two rejections before it cleared, so plan on a wait rather than a same-day start. That wait is the reason this is worth paying someone for.

Getting set up as a business

If you are still operating as yourself rather than as a company, that is worth sorting before any of the rest of this.

I went through the whole thing in Georgia this year: the entity, a registered agent so my home address stayed off the public record, the federal tax number, the city occupation tax certificate, the landlord consent my lease turned out to require, and a business bank account. I know the order it goes in, what each step costs, and the two or three places it quietly stalls.

Straight with you, and this one matters. What I can do is sit with you and walk through it while you file it, in your own accounts and under your own name, and tell you what each screen is asking for. What I will not do is file it for you, pick your entity type, or write your operating agreement. Those are legal decisions and you want an attorney for them. Anyone who offers to just handle all of it for a fee is doing something they probably should not be.

It is on this list because a website, a Google listing and a phone number all want a business name and an address attached to them. Doing this first saves redoing the other four.
