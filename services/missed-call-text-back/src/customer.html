The call you miss is the job you lose

Most people who call a small business and get voicemail do not leave one. They hang up and call the next name on the list. You never find out it happened.

What this does

When a call comes in and nobody picks up, the caller gets a text from your business within seconds. They can answer it right there. The conversation carries on in the one place people actually reply, and you answer when you get a minute.

A few seconds after the call ends
Got your call. This is Isaiah with Isaiah Smith Films. Text me what you need right here and I'll get back to you. Photos of your space help.
Hey, need video for my shop. Free Thursday?
The reply lands on your phone. Their number is already in the thread.

What it is not

It is not a chatbot and nobody is pretending to be you. It is one automatic first text that holds the door open, and after that it is just you texting a customer.

Why me

I run this exact setup on my own business line. I built it, registered it and tested it on myself before offering it to anyone, so when I tell you how it behaves I am telling you what I have watched it do.

Three ways to set up the number

Which one fits depends on where your business is today. We work this out together on the first call, and none of them is more work for you than the others.

A new business number

For anyone still running the business off a personal cell. You get a real local number that belongs to the business. It goes on your website, your Google listing and your truck, and calls still reach your phone. Your personal number stays personal.

Best when you want the business to look like a business. The one cost is updating the number where you have already published it.

Keep the number you already have

For a business with a line customers know and a sign that already has it printed on. Your existing line forwards to the system only when nobody answers. Callers dial the same number they always have, and nothing about your current phone changes.

One thing to know up front: the text-back reaches them from the service number, not the number they dialed. Most customers do not blink at this, but you should hear it from me now rather than find out later.

Move your number over

Your number itself transfers across, so calls and texts both live on it and the customer sees one number the whole way through. This is the cleanest experience on their end.

It takes longer to arrange and there is a switchover day to plan around, so it suits a business that is settled and not about to rebrand.

What I need to switch it on

Your greeting. In your own voice if you want it, and I will write one and record it with you if you would rather not write it yourself.
The first text, in your words. What it says, and what you want the customer to send back. Asking for a photo works better than asking for details.
Where replies land. Your cell, or a phone in the shop, or both.
Carrier registration. Every US business sending automated texts has to be registered with the phone carriers first. I fill out that paperwork and deal with the reviewers. Texting switches on once it clears, so plan on a short wait rather than a same-day start.
