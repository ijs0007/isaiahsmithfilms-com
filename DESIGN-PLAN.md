## Colour, every value from a named frame

  --paper   #CED3DF   f600, the prescription pad         page ground
  --chart   #CCE0E4   f456, the chart light box          raised surfaces, the lighter paper
  --red     #6E2A2A   f744, her apron                    the one action colour
  --warm    #DBB094   f288, the bare bulb                the warm light, used sparingly
  --wood    #694337   f672, the exam room panelling      rules, borders, secondary text
  --ink     #0C0A0E   f288, the darkest corner of frame  type

Note what this does to the earlier build. The film is COOL paper against WARM wood. I had
built warm paper, which is the source backwards. #CED3DF is bluer and greyer than any cream,
and it is the actual colour of the pad the doctor writes the prescription on.

The red is #6E2A2A, much deeper than the #A0363A I had been using. It is her apron. It is also
the colour of the line on the falling chart.

## Type, three candidates, pick one

A. PRINT SHOP        Archivo Expanded  +  Newsreader
   The only type inside the film is institutional print: a chart header, a pharmacy pad, a
   name badge. Archivo is a grotesque drawn for small print sizes and its expanded cut has
   the squareness of a form heading. Newsreader carries body text like a printed page.
   The argument: the page is set in the same two kinds of type the film's own props are.

B. THE COLLISION     Fraunces  +  Public Sans
   Fraunces is a soft serif with a wonk axis, warm and slightly old fashioned. Public Sans is
   the US government's civic sans, plain to the point of being official.
   The argument: warm-human against cold-institutional is the film's entire joke, and this is
   that joke in two typefaces.

C. CONTINUITY        Lato Black, flush set  +  Spectral
   The film's end cards are already set in Lato Black, flush to both edges. Carry it onto the
   page and the scroll from film to page reads as one object rather than two.
   The honest downside: Lato is a common face and all the distinction would have to come from
   the flush setting. This is the safest and the least characterful of the three.

Alignment: one left edge for every section at every width, already measured and proven.
The right side stays open on purpose. Nothing is centred.

## Principles

1. The film is the proof. Nothing else on the page has to argue.
2. The page inherits the film's own light. Cool paper, warm wood, one deep red.
3. Emphasis is carried by scale, never by colour. Colour means "this is an action."
4. Prices are stated plainly and early. Hiding them is what everyone else does.
5. Nothing floats. Rules and edges instead of cards and shadows.
